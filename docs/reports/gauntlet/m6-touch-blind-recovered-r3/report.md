# Independent blind visual review — round 3

**Choice: A.** Across the supplied white, gray, arena and smoke frames, A keeps the joystick ring/thumb, labels and button boundaries easier to find, with its clearest advantage on white and at the small clamp. Its dark plates and light/dark edge protection also hold up over textured and shadowed areas, while the red/gold held treatment and muted zero-count FRAG remain distinguishable.

**Single largest remaining gap:** small-clamp text packing and size. RELOAD and SMOKE crowd or extend beyond their circular plate, and the muted FRAG label/icon is especially hard to identify at a glance; a visible zero badge communicates depletion more readily than the tiny dim utility name does. A improves the foreground protection but does not solve that typography/layout limitation.

**Regressions and tradeoff:** I saw no missing control/icon, changed label, displaced control, or mismatched idle/held depiction in A relative to B in these frames. A's heavier outlines make the smallest label/rim combinations busier, and its darker plates and more solid thumb conceal more scene detail than B; this is most apparent in the maximum-size mobile cluster over the right container and in the large joystick thumb. The joystick's interior outside the thumb stays transparent, and the central view remains open in these fixtures. These are visible scene-transparency costs, not evidence of an observed missed enemy or gameplay failure.

**Coverage and limits:** I read the neutral README and manifest first and inspected all 64 original PNGs individually with original-resolution image views: all 32 matched pairs, both 960 × 720 and 1280 × 720 dimensions, both clamps, all four backgrounds, and idle/held states. No source key, external code, analyzer, OCR, engine, native capture or other review was used. Static pixels establish appearance only: they cannot establish actual hit regions, finger reach or occlusion, safe-area behavior on a device, simultaneous input correctness, responsiveness, motion/glance performance, display/glare effects, accessibility conformance or shipped-title parity. This preference does not approve mobile/physical release quality.
