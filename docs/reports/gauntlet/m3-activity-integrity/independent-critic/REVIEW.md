I ran `adversarial_probe.gd` against a separate sparse worktree of the exact
candidate product. `adversarial.json` retains concrete input queue, ACK, actor
look, activity timestamp, accumulated time and eligibility observations. It
checks these cases independently of the builder fixtures:

- A queued real look gains no time until consumed; a later duplicate neutral
  cannot erase that immutable real look. A later stale opposite look cannot
  renew unchanged consumed input.
- Returning from a nonzero look to zero is still a real qualifying aim change.
  An earlier neutral sequence consumes before a queued actual look; only the
  second step gains its activity. Same-packet duplicates retain the stream's
  existing last-staged-value behavior and activity matches that consumed value.
- Epoch reset clears queued aim; old-epoch replay cannot affect current neutral
  consumption. A malformed tail rejects the whole packet. Ten skipped aim
  commands behind the backlog cap gain no activity.
- The real disconnect handler clears the old peer queue. Rebinding the actor to
  another peer with neutral input cannot consume the former aim; ranked
  eligibility remains false after the disconnect. This is a synthetic handler
  check, not a new full ENet reconnect campaign.
- Finite pitch clamps before use. Equivalent wrapped yaw and clamped pitch do
  not renew activity; a real change after normalization does. Waiting/buy/post
  and dead actors retain existing consumed-input activity eligibility. Dead
  input is intentionally eligible although the dead actor pose does not turn.
