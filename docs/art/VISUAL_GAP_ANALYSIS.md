# Visual gap analysis: SIGHTBREAK against Counter-Strike 2's visual standard

**Status:** working document for the graphics initiative branch `claude/cs2-fidelity-art-production`. It is not a release claim.

## Reference access (read first)

- **No CS2 imagery was studied in this session.**
  - counter-strike.net and every page fetch failed from the build container (proxy 403; WebFetch DNS failure).
  - The billed scraping connector was not authorised and was not used.
- **What the principles below rest on.** They come only from web-search summaries of press coverage and patch notes. The sources are listed in [`tools/art/REFERENCES.md`](../../tools/art/REFERENCES.md).
- **Verification still owed.** Every "CS2" statement here is a paraphrase of those reports, not an observation. A person with the game should check the comparisons before they drive design decisions.
- **No CS2 content is used.** No CS2 mesh, texture, map, character, animation, audio or shader was downloaded, traced or recreated. Every SIGHTBREAK asset on this branch is generated by code in `tools/art/`.

## Principles CS2 is reported to rely on

1. **Physically based materials.** Albedo, roughness and metalness are separated, with reflections from a unified lighting model.
2. **Lighting and readability are tuned together.** Patch notes repeatedly "adjust lighting in certain areas to improve player readability", and remove or tweak models that hurt it.
3. **High-detail first-person weapons and hands.** Machined forms, real texel density and wear, with skins that sit on top of the material detail rather than replacing it.
4. **Volumetric smokes.** They fill space, react to lighting and gunfire, and briefly clear when shot.
5. **Characters with clear team silhouettes** at gameplay distance.

## Realistic ceiling on this stack

SIGHTBREAK uses Godot's GL Compatibility renderer on desktop and mobile. That rules out several of the techniques the reports attribute to CS2's look:
- screen-space reflections;
- SDFGI and screen-space indirect lighting;
- SSAO;
- the volumetric fog that the Forward+ renderer provides.

What remains attainable is:
- strong PBR assets with baked occlusion;
- authored lighting with baked lightmaps (LightmapGI does work in Compatibility);
- reflection probes;
- careful tonemapping;
- good effects;
- readability tuning.

A desktop-only tier on Forward+ is possible, but the directive forbids making mobile depend on it. So the comparison target is CS2's asset quality and readability discipline, not its renderer feature list.
